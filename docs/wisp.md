## Why you need one

- **The FTC Safeguards Rule covers you.** The rule (16 CFR Part 314) lists tax preparation services and mortgage brokers among its examples of financial institutions, in [§ 314.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/314.2).
- **The program must be written.** [§ 314.3(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/314.3) requires a comprehensive information security program "written in one or more readily accessible parts", with the elements listed in [§ 314.4](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/314.4).
- **The IRS expects it too.** The IRS and the Security Summit remind tax professionals that federal law requires them to create and maintain a written information security plan ([IR-2026-92](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-security-summit-remind-tax-pros-they-need-a-written-information-security-plan-to-protect-client-data)).
- **Breach reporting.** Since May 13, 2024, covered firms must notify the FTC no later than 30 days after discovering a security event involving the unencrypted information of at least 500 consumers ([§ 314.4(j)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/314.4), [§ 314.5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/314.5)).

Smaller firms get some relief: firms holding customer information on fewer than 5,000 consumers are exempt from the written risk assessment, the testing requirement in § 314.4(d)(2), the written incident response plan and the annual written report ([§ 314.6](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/16/314.6)). They still need the written program itself.

## What you get

- **Your WISP**, written for your firm's size, staff, software and the way you actually handle client data, covering the elements of § 314.4
- **Qualified Individual** designation: who oversees the program
- **Written risk assessment** and **incident response plan**, if your firm needs them (or as good practice if it doesn't)
- **Service provider list**: your tax software, cloud storage and IT provider, with what each one handles
- **Staff training record** and a one-page summary staff can follow
- **A gap list**: anything the rule expects that you don't do yet, such as multi-factor authentication or encryption, in plain English with what to fix first
